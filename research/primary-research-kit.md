# CampusRun: primary research kit (campus-level validation)

The brief requires at least one form of campus-level validation, and slide 1 has four orange `[__]` boxes waiting for **your real numbers**. This kit gives you everything needed to collect them at GIM in 3–4 days. Do not submit with placeholders, and do not estimate them.

Run **at least two** of the four methods. Survey + smoke test is the strongest pair, because one shows stated demand and the other shows real behaviour.

---

## Method 1: Student survey (Google Form, target n ≥ 120)

Share it on batch WhatsApp groups (PGDM 1st + 2nd year, hostels) with a 48-hour deadline. Keep it under 3 minutes.

1. Year / programme · Hostel block
2. In the last 30 days, how many times did you eat food **not** from the mess? (0 / 1–3 / 4–8 / 9–15 / 15+)
3. Where did that food come from? (campus canteen / food court / someone drove to town / delivery app / friends brought it / other)
4. Have you tried to order on Swiggy or another app to campus and found it **not serviceable** or too slow? (Yes, often / Yes, once or twice / Never tried / No, it worked)
5. If delivery reached campus, how far is the hand-off point from your room, and how many minutes do you spend walking and waiting? (number)
6. When are you most likely to want outside food? (lunch / evening snacks / dinner / after 11 PM)
7. What do you usually spend per outside meal? (< ₹150 / 150–250 / 250–400 / 400+)
8. Which of these frustrates you most? Rank: not serviceable · delivery and other fees · waiting at the gate · minimum order · coordinating group orders · mess quality
9. **Concept test.** Show the CampusRun home screen (`assets/phone-home.png`) and one sentence: "3 fixed delivery runs a day to a campus drop point, ₹15 run fee, no small-cart fee." How likely are you to use it at least once a week? (1–5)
10. Would you wait for a fixed slot (e.g. 8:30 PM) if it saved you ₹40+ per meal? (Yes / Maybe / No)
11. How often would you order with roommates or your wing to split costs? (Never / Sometimes / Usually)
12. Would you use an AI assistant that builds a group cart within a budget ("dinner for 4 under ₹900")? (Yes / Maybe / No)
13. Would you work as a paid Campus Captain for ~30 minutes per run? (Yes / Maybe / No)
14. Anything else? (open)

**How the answers map to the deck**
- Slide 1 `[__]% tried ordering and failed or gave up` = Q4 "Yes, often" + "Yes, once or twice"
- Slide 1 `[__] min` = median of Q5
- Slide 1 `[__]% would order from a fixed slot to save ₹40+` = Q10 "Yes"
- Survey n = number of responses
- For Q&A: Q6 (slot timing), Q7 (AOV assumption), Q11 (pod share), Q13 (Captain supply)

## Method 2: Student interviews (8–10 × 15 min)

Pick a spread: day-1 first-years, 2nd-years, someone who has a vehicle, someone who doesn't, a hostel rep or mess committee member, a canteen operator, and if you can a security guard at the gate.

1. Walk me through the last time you wanted food that wasn't from the mess. What happened?
2. What did you end up eating? What did it cost, and how long did it take?
3. When do groups of you order together? How is that coordinated today, and who pays?
4. What would make you trust a delivery that arrives at a fixed time instead of "in 30 minutes"?
5. (Show the prototype.) What's confusing? What's missing? Would you use it this week?
6. (Gate/security) How many delivery hand-offs a night? Any problems? Would one drop shelf help?

Record one **quote per interview** worth putting on a slide or saying in the video.

---

## Method 3: Gate observation (3 evenings, 7:30–11:30 PM)

Log it in a sheet with these columns:

| Time | Delivery or pickup? | App / source | # students collecting | Minutes the rider waited | Minutes the student walked or waited | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Output for the deck: hand-offs per evening, peak window, average rider wait, average student walk/wait.

---

## Method 4: Smoke test, the strongest evidence (1 evening)

Run a manual "CampusRun" once:

1. Post in hostel groups at 6:00 PM: "Pooled dinner run from [restaurant cluster] → main gate at 9:00 PM. Order closes 8:00 PM. One trip, split delivery cost." Use a Google Form with the menu and UPI pre-payment, or a WhatsApp poll.
2. Two teammates (or a hired cab/bike) collect at the restaurant and drive to campus.
3. Hand over at the gate with names / last-4-digit check (a manual OTP).
4. Measure: orders received, conversion from views, delivery cost per order, on-time arrival, repeat-interest poll the next morning.

The headline line for slide 1: "One manual run took **[__] orders in [__] minutes**; cost per order fell from ₹[__] to ₹[__]."

---

## Ethics and consent
Tell respondents why you are asking, keep responses anonymous, and do not publish raw data. Competition rules forbid publishing the prototype or materials without Swiggy's approval.
